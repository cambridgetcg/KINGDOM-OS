## The Fly 2026 facts that change our plans

- **GPUs are DEAD** — deprecated 2026-07-31, gone after Aug 1. Never build chain/soma/inference on Fly GPUs; use external GPU clouds.
- **Sprites** (new Jan 2026, sprites.dev — separate product) — hardware-isolated persistent sandboxes, **$0 compute when idle**, 300ms copy-on-write checkpoints + instant rollback, wake in 1–12s. **This is the answer to agenttool Horizon C (trusted-tier hosted runtime)** and to per-citizen agent sandboxes — better than Machines autostop for that shape.
- **Volumes are single-host, no replication** — host dies → lose everything since the last snapshot. Fly says run ≥2 machines+volumes OR external backup. Our mainnet chain is **one node, one volume** → real risk.
- **Tigris** object storage — S3-compatible, **zero egress fees**, 5GB free then $0.02/GB. GA. Native `fly storage create`.
- **Unmanaged Fly Postgres is deprecated** ("This Is Not Managed Postgres"); **Managed Postgres (MPG) is GA** ($38/mo basic). Our SaaS -db apps are on borrowed time if unmanaged.
- **Upstash Redis is command-metered** — BullMQ polls hard → pay-as-you-go bills explode; use a fixed plan or self-host Valkey (~$2–4/mo). (agenttool currently runs `AGENTTOOL_DISABLE_WORKERS=1` — no live BullMQ cost today.)
- Region Consolidation (Sep 2025) closed 17 regions → ~19 remain. All our apps are lhr/cdg — safe.

## Online salvage contract (revision 2026-09-08, installed that day; amended 2026-10-19)

- Both runners archive the running node's `data` and `config`. **Tar exit 0 and 1 are `RAW_UNVERIFIED`**, including when the object has positive size. A live LevelDB copy can be inconsistent even when tar reports no changes; “resync the tail” is not a recovery guarantee. These archives are not a complete coherent validator home.
- Tar exit >1 is `FAILED`, and upload is `NOT_ATTEMPTED`. Tar/curl/SSH results and the independent object-presence check are retained separately; neither a successful SSH command nor stored bytes erase a failed capture or upload. The remote runner requires exactly one bounded protocol record; missing, oversized or malformed output fails closed. Fly stdout banners also fail closed rather than being silently ignored.
- Per-chain JSON reports `capture_status` (`RAW_UNVERIFIED`/`FAILED`), `upload_status` (`PRESENT`/`FAILED`/`NOT_ATTEMPTED`), numeric exit codes where known, and independent `object_status`. A failed object check means unknown presence, not proven absence. Transport/protocol failure may leave an object in storage; it is not cleaned up or promoted. `recovery_status` is always `NOT_ASSESSED`; `retention_status` is always `DISABLED`.
- The remote runner also reports `upload_http`: curl's `%{http_code}` for the PUT, `0` when no HTTP response arrived, and `null` when no upload was attempted or curl wrote anything but three digits. It sits beside `upload_exit` and never replaces it. Before 2026-10-19 curl's output went to `/dev/null`, so a `curl 22` (for example mainnet on 2026-10-12) cannot be told apart after the fact.
- The PUT URL is presigned for **12 hours**, before the remote tar starts. At 2 hours it expired before curl began on CPU-credit-throttled nights: once a Fly shared-CPU machine has used its burst credit (about 1.5 days after a restart), testnet's tar alone took 2h05–2h19, and 25 of 40 testnet nights from 2026-09-09 failed, 21 of them with `curl 22`. The longer URL stays visible in local and remote process arguments for the length of the run, and a replay within those hours could overwrite only that night's object key.
- Exit **3** means all requested salvage captures landed but remain unverified; **1** means an operational failure; **2** means invalid usage/configuration, rejected before effects. There is **no exit-0 verified-success path**, no verified receipt, and no advancement of verified-backup freshness. Logs omit raw subprocess output, presigned URLs, credentials and archive contents.
- **No remote listing or deletion in either implementation.** Every existing generation, including legacy and known-good objects, is preserved. Unique capture suffixes avoid routine same-second overwrites. Only newly created private temporary files are cleaned up. Storage will grow without bound until a separately reviewed retention policy exists; upload success must never trigger pruning.
- The in-node alternative requires tar/zstd, AWS CLI and explicitly provisioned credentials; its default home is `/data/.zeroned`. It performs no snapshot/export fallback. Genesis export/import is diagnostic-only, not validator recovery.

### Amendment 2026-10-20 (ledger `zc-chain-backup-runner`; an agent's call under Yu's 2026-10-20 delegation)

- **Rollout is testnet first.** `ROLLOUT_CHAINS` in the runner was `["zerone-testnet-1"]`, and mainnet's remote command stayed byte-identical to 2026-10-19 (a test pins that capture's sha256). Mainnet joined in a one-line change once this was merged and testnet had a clean night; see the 2026-10-21 amendment below.
- **Key-free new archives (rollout chains, and always in the in-node alternative).** tar leaves out `priv_validator_key.json` and `node_key.json`, and keeps `data/priv_validator_state.json`. Older archives, the Fly volume and its snapshots still hold both keys, so no copy is lost. Restoring a key-bearing archive onto a second machine would double-sign the single validator. Each log line says what the capture was asked to archive: `key_files: "EXCLUDED"|"INCLUDED"`. Deleting old archives and escrowing the key are not part of this; see ledger `zc-backup-archive-retention`.
- **Low-disk pre-flight skip (rollout chains).** Before tar, the remote reads `df -Pk` for its staging directory. The run is skipped when free space is below the last landed archive plus 1 GiB, or when free space cannot be read. A skip leaves the rootfs untouched, prints `CHAIN_BACKUP_V2 skip=LOW_DISK free_kib=… need_kib=…`, and is logged as `capture_status: "SKIPPED"`, `error: "SKIPPED_LOW_DISK"`, exit 1 (red). The bound comes from the landed marker below. Until a chain has a marker, the bound is the 1 GiB margin alone. Replacing staging with a streamed or multipart upload is a separate change, rehearsed on testnet for several nights before mainnet.
- **Per-chain landed marker.** A marker is written only when an upload lands cleanly (no error, object present). It lives at `~/.kingdom/state/chain-backup/<chain>.landed`, written atomically as `{"protocol":"kingdom.chain-backup.landed/1","chain","landed_at","bytes","capture_status":"RAW_UNVERIFIED","key_files"}`. A marker that cannot be written is reported as `landed_marker: "FAILED"`, never hidden.
- **Pulse has a third state.** `amber_exit: [3]` turns the job's exit 3 into amber, "captured, unverified". It is no longer DOWN. Each chain's marker is a `file:` heartbeat with `amber_note`, so fresh means amber and older than 30 h or missing means red. Amber is not an alert. Green stays reserved for a verified recovery, which nothing provides yet.

### Amendment 2026-10-28 (ledger `zc-chain-backup-runner`): streamed multipart capture, testnet rehearsal

- **Why.** Mainnet has not captured since 2026-10-21: it skipped on 2026-10-22, -23, -27 and -28 (the 10-24 to 10-26 runs failed at SSH, before any pre-flight). Testnet skipped for the first time on 2026-10-28. Both skip because each landed archive (6,876,956,202 bytes for testnet on 2026-10-27) plus the 1 GiB margin now exceeds the rootfs's free space (7,688,964 KiB on testnet, 7,688,868 KiB on mainnet, unchanged night to night). A skip writes no marker, so the bound never falls again and the skips would never end on their own.
- **What.** For chains in `STREAM_CHAINS`, which is `["zerone-testnet-1"]` only, the Mac creates one S3 multipart upload and presigns 64 UploadPart URLs (12 h). The remote pipes `tar czf -` through `head -c 256 MiB` into one private part file at a time and `curl -T`s each part. Tigris answers 411 to a chunked PUT, so each part needs a Content-Length. Peak rootfs use is one 256 MiB part instead of the whole archive, so the streamed pre-flight bound is one part plus 1 GiB (1,310,720 KiB). Parts are capped at 64, a 16 GiB ceiling. The remote prints one `CHAIN_BACKUP_V3 tar=… parts=… bytes=… upload=… http=…` record. The Mac lists that upload's parts, requires exactly the reported parts in order (every part but the last a full part), requires `bytes` to equal the parts' sum, completes the upload, and requires the object's size to equal that same sum before it writes the landed marker. The object key and format are unchanged: one `.tar.gz` per capture.
- **Why `bytes`, and why GNU head (review of #124).** Cutting parts is sound only if `head -c` reads exactly one part from the pipe. GNU head does. BSD/macOS head reads a whole stdio buffer and drops the excess, yet every part still looks whole (full, full, short last, then empty), so ListParts, Complete and the size check would all pass on a corrupt archive. So the remote refuses the capture (`CHAIN_BACKUP_V3 refuse=HEAD_NOT_GNU`, logged as `REFUSED_HEAD_NOT_GNU`, no marker) unless `head --version` starts `head (GNU coreutils) `. And `tee /dev/fd/3` hands every byte of the tar stream to `wc -c`, a count independent of head, proven on five bytes before tar starts (`refuse=NO_BYTE_COUNT` otherwise). A count that is missing or differs from the parts' sum leaves the upload `OPEN`, never completed, with `STREAM_MISMATCH`. The count is logged as `stream_bytes`.
- **What it does not do.** A torn stream (a failed part, tar above 1, too many parts, a skip, a refusal, a count mismatch) is never completed into a truncated archive, and its upload is left `OPEN`, never aborted here. Those open uploads hold their parts in storage (up to about 7 GB for a torn night) until they are aborted; that cleanup belongs to `zc-backup-archive-retention`. The only listing is of the parts of the upload this run created; no stored generation is listed or deleted. Exit codes, the amber pulse and the per-chain marker are unchanged, and the exit stays 1 on any night either chain skips or fails.
- **Mainnet stays staged.** Its remote command is byte-identical to installed runner `524a73c5…` (a test pins its sha256), so mainnet keeps skipping until it joins `STREAM_CHAINS`. That is a one-line follow-up after several clean testnet nights (`capture_mode: "STREAMED_MULTIPART"`, `multipart_status: "COMPLETED"`, `landed_marker: "WRITTEN"`, `stream_bytes` equal to the marker's bytes), and after a read-only `gzip -t` and `tar -tzf` of the first streamed testnet object has passed.

For coherent whole-home recovery, use the existing Zerone `tools/validator-home-manifest` and `docs/UPGRADE_AND_INCIDENT_OPERATIONS.md` in the selected release checkout. The manifest tool is **integrity-only**: matching self-hashes or an operator boolean do not prove compatible application/checkpoint recovery or independently established signer fencing. Those controls and a restore rehearsal require separately selected evidence and explicit operational authorisation. This patch neither creates another recovery authority nor promotes online archives to verified backups.

### Replacement-only procedure (source tooling; activation not claimed here)

The approved replacement scope is only `~/.kingdom/bin/chain-backup-remote.ts`, plus exclusive local rollback/staging artifacts beside it. The implementation/preparation tests did not change the installed runner. Its legacy pruning persists until the parent/operator completes the authorized replacement; an already-running legacy invocation is not retroactively changed.

Before running, review this installer and its isolated tests. Use an unprivileged owner session on the awake Mac, in a quiet interval away from **03:33 local** and wake catch-up. Check that no known local/manual/remote capture is in progress; if that cannot be established, defer rather than stopping anything. The installer samples the registered `gui/<uid>/com.kingdom.chain-backup` job twice, two seconds apart, then again immediately before final file checks. It accepts only the expected plist/program/arguments, `state = not running`, `active count = 0`, and no PID. PID-only `pgrep -f` checks the three known backup script names; errors, unknown metadata, or any match defer. Subprocess output is bounded to 128 KiB and five seconds per observation and is never printed. No raw process arguments are requested from `ps` or logged. The installer refuses 03:18–03:48 inclusive; the human assertion also covers wake catch-up, which it cannot measure.

These are **observations, not a cross-host lock, service fence, or proof of orphan absence**. An invocation may start after the final observation. A private transaction marker serializes cooperating installer instances only, not captures. Controlled ownership and an operator quiet window are required; the final identity-check/rename pair is not a compare-and-swap against hostile same-UID writers.

Run from a trusted PATH/interpreter environment (Python 3 standard library plus Darwin `libSystem` copyfile/ACL/xattr APIs; no package installation). `--home` is mandatory and explicit: inherited `HOME` never chooses the write target. Use the absolute, non-symlink script path. Check parents/source/destination are regular owner-controlled paths; unsafe links, multiple hardlinks, group/world write permissions, foreign ownership, unsupported parent allow-ACLs or unpreservable metadata cause refusal. ACL refusal uses native Darwin entry tags and permission masks, not display-text matching: every parent allow entry is unsupported (even read-only or inherit-only), and file allow entries may grant only read, execute, read-attributes, read-extended-attributes, read-security or synchronize. Mutation/unknown grants and ACL inspection failures defer; deny entries cannot hide a later allow grant. Harmless file read ACLs remain preserved. Root-owned sticky ancestor directories are permitted, but the actual home/source/destination directories must be owner-controlled and non-shared. The existing plist must still bind the expected wrapper with **RunAtLoad=false** and **03:33**; wrapper/plist bytes and identities are checked again and never written in this action.

After source review, the parent can perform the selected replacement with this exact invocation (not a test or a capture command). The digests pin the 2026-10-19 replacement (2026-09-08 runner → 12-hour URL runner); for any later one, recompute both with `shasum -a 256` over the source and `~/.kingdom/bin/chain-backup-remote.ts`:

```sh
PATH=/usr/bin:/bin:/usr/sbin:/sbin /bin/bash /Users/yournameisai/Desktop/kingdom-os/bin/chain-backup-install.sh replace-runner \
  --home /Users/yournameisai \
  --expected-source 41996699510e03235071246958110a9a4bf611e9c24727d3dad217db9a7a847c \
  --expected-installed 826123f748ab0386be34db00e9455d17ff1565204b4580e880ba69a5b246b139 \
  --quiet-window-confirmed
```

Expected result: installer exit **0** with a `REPLACED` JSON receipt, new installed digest equal to the pinned source, and preserved old digest in an exclusively created `.chain-backup-rollback-<random>/chain-backup-remote.ts`. Native fd-based copying plus verification preserves owner/group/mode, mtime, flags, extended ACL and xattr values. Inode, ctime and birth time are not retained across replacement. Files and directories are fsynced, final source/destination/stage identities and hashes rechecked, and a single same-directory rename publishes the runner. `prepared.json` means only prepared; `receipt.json` records completed replacement. These are **replacement receipts, never verified recovery receipts**. Rollback directories are local source artifacts, not chain homes or stored archives.

A failure exits **1** with a bounded error and `runner_replaced` state. A failure after rename can leave the new runner installed; do not infer that nonzero exit restored the old file. Preserve and inspect any rollback artifact/transaction marker rather than retrying blindly. No automatic rollback exists. **Never roll back because a later capture exits 3**: that is the intended `RAW_UNVERIFIED` result and restoring the old runner would restore pruning. Recheck the installed digest, wrapper/plist identity, job binding and schedule after an authorized replacement without loading/unloading anything or taking a manual capture.

Future scheduled raw captures retain every generation. Storage grows without bound; the current runner enforces **no archive-size, storage-budget or capture-runtime ceiling**. Storage budgeting, delivered alerts, coherent whole-home recovery/fencing evidence, history/capacity planning and separately authorized launch/cutover decisions remain operational gates. Replacement performs no credential lookup, SSH/S3/network call, service-control call, capture, restore, stored-object deletion or chain stop.

For a separately authorized first schedule installation only, the formerly implicit installer invocation is now `bash /absolute/path/to/kingdom-os/bin/chain-backup-install.sh schedule-install --home /absolute/owner/home`. That legacy action creates/overwrites runner, wrapper and plist and bootouts/bootstraps launchd. Its generated Bash wrapper safely quotes and exports the explicit `--home` target; inherited `HOME` cannot choose another runner or credential home. Home validation rejects XML metacharacters/quotes, newline/carriage-return/tab characters and backslashes (Bun normalizes backslashes in module paths); unsupported paths are rejected before effects. Synthetic two-home execution tests cover spaces and shell metacharacters using inert programs, not a real capture. This action is neither a replacement fallback nor authorized by the replacement-only selection.
